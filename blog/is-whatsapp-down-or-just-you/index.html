Is WhatsApp down, or is it just you? How to check in two minutes

Published November 21, 2026

Your messages sit on a little clock, WhatsApp Web says it's connecting, and the person you're waiting on hasn't answered in an hour. Before you restart everything you own, spend two minutes finding out whether WhatsApp itself is having a bad day. It does happen: on Friday, November 13, 2026, problem reports for WhatsApp stayed at outage level for almost 18 hours, according to the outage tracker IsItDownRightNow, with spikes in the UK, the Netherlands, Italy, Germany, Spain and several other countries.

Most days, though, the problem is closer to home. Here's how to tell which one you have.

Step 1: Read what your messages are telling you

WhatsApp's help center spells out what each mark means, and it's the fastest clue you have.

  • A clock next to your message means it hasn't left your device yet. That points at your connection, or at WhatsApp not accepting messages.
  • One grey tick means the message left your phone but hasn't reached the other person's device. Their phone may be off, out of data, or they may have blocked you. One stuck chat with a single tick is almost never an outage. (More on that in why a WhatsApp message shows one grey tick.)
  • Two ticks mean delivered. Blue means read.

If only one chat is stuck and the rest are fine, WhatsApp is working. If every chat shows a clock, keep going.

Step 2: Check your own connection first

These checks come straight from WhatsApp's help center and take under a minute:

  1. Open any website in your phone's browser. If it doesn't load, it's your internet, not WhatsApp.
  2. Turn airplane mode on, wait a few seconds, and turn it off.
  3. Switch from Wi-Fi to mobile data, or the other way round. If WhatsApp works on one and not the other, the problem is that network.
  4. Restart the phone.

If websites and other apps work but WhatsApp alone won't connect on mobile data, WhatsApp suggests asking your mobile provider to check your APN settings, because WhatsApp uses non-web data channels that some carrier setups block.

On a computer, WhatsApp Web shows a yellow Computer not connected banner when your computer has lost its connection. Office and university Wi-Fi can block WhatsApp too. WhatsApp's fix list for Web says to try another network or a phone hotspot, refresh the page, and clear the browser's cache and cookies if it still won't connect.

Step 3: Ask one other person

The quickest outage test is a person on a different network. Text or call a friend or coworker another way and ask them to send you a WhatsApp message, or ask whether their WhatsApp is working. If theirs is also stuck, and they're on different internet from you, it's very likely WhatsApp.

Step 4: Check outside sources

As The Sunday Guardian noted during the November 13 reports, WhatsApp doesn't offer a public real-time status dashboard for the regular app. These are the places to look instead:

  • WhatsApp's account on X (@WhatsApp). During the big Meta outage on December 11, 2024, WhatsApp posted there that it was aware of issues, then posted again when service was back, as TechCrunch reported. Don't read too much into silence there, though.
  • Outage trackers such as Downdetector or IsItDownRightNow. They count problem reports from users. Trackers get some reports every day, so look for a spike well above the usual level, in several countries at once. As IsItDownRightNow notes on its own page, reports show that many people had trouble at the same time, not what caused it.
  • Meta Status. This tracks Meta's business products, such as Ads Manager and the WhatsApp Business API. It can help if your business uses the API, but it isn't a status page for the regular app.

If it is an outage: what to do on WhatsApp Web

Don't log out, uninstall or clear your data during an outage. None of that brings WhatsApp back faster, and logging out of WhatsApp Web means linking your computer again, which needs WhatsApp's servers to be working. Leave the tab open.

A few things are worth doing while you wait:

  • Reach urgent contacts another way. Call, text or email anyone who is waiting on you, so a missed reply doesn't become a missed appointment.
  • Re-read busy chats afterwards. Once service is back, scroll up through your busiest chats and groups instead of reading only the newest message, so you don't miss something that came in while you were stuck.
  • Check for messages stuck on a clock. If your own message still shows a clock once everything else works, delete it and send it again.

If it isn't an outage: fixes for WhatsApp Web

If other people's WhatsApp works and yours doesn't, the problem is on your side. These guides cover the common cases:

  • Web stuck on the green loading bar: WhatsApp Web stuck on "Loading your chats"
  • Logged out over and over: WhatsApp Web keeps logging you out
  • Messages arrive but you get no alert: WhatsApp Web notifications not working in Chrome
  • The Windows app won't open: WhatsApp Desktop not working on Windows

One more thing worth knowing: WhatsApp Web and Desktop keep working when your phone is offline, but WhatsApp's help center says linked devices get logged out if you don't use your phone for over 14 days. If your computer suddenly shows the QR code again after a long trip away from your phone, that's why. Open WhatsApp on the phone and link the computer again.

Quick checklist

  1. Clock on every chat, or one grey tick on one chat? One chat is a person problem, not an outage.
  2. Can you load a website? If not, fix your internet.
  3. Try the other network (Wi-Fi or mobile data, or a hotspot for your computer).
  4. Ask someone on a different network whether their WhatsApp works.
  5. Look at @WhatsApp on X and an outage tracker for a spike in many countries.
  6. Outage confirmed? Leave WhatsApp Web open, reach urgent people another way, and re-read busy chats when it comes back.
