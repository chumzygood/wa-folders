Unofficial WhatsApp desktop apps like ZapFast: are they safe?

Published November 24, 2026

An unofficial WhatsApp desktop app such as ZapFast can be fast and well made, but WhatsApp's own Help Center says that linking your account to an unofficial app violates its Terms of Service and can get the account temporarily or permanently banned, so the risk is to your account, not only to your computer.

ZapFast got attention this month as a lighter alternative to WhatsApp Web and the WhatsApp desktop apps. Below is what it is, what WhatsApp says about apps like it, and the official options if memory use is the reason you are looking. Everything here comes from ZapFast's README and website and WhatsApp's Help Center, all read on November 24, 2026. We have not installed ZapFast or linked an account to it.

What ZapFast is

According to its GitHub README, ZapFast is an MIT-licensed WhatsApp client for Linux, macOS and Windows, written in Rust. It has no browser engine. It links to your phone as a companion device, the same way WhatsApp Web does, through an open-source library called whatsapp-rust.

Its website lists what it does: text, replies, edits, reactions, voice messages, files and stickers, one-to-one voice and video calls, several linked numbers in one window, and a local chat archive encrypted with a key in your system keyring. It also lists what it doesn't do yet, including status posts, communities, channels and payments.

The headline number is memory. ZapFast says that in its own Linux test it uses about 200 MB of RAM when idle, against 1.13 GB for WhatsApp Web and its Chromium processes. That is the developer's measurement, not ours.

What WhatsApp says about unofficial apps

WhatsApp's Help Center page About unofficial WhatsApp apps is direct:

  • Unofficial apps are versions of WhatsApp made by third parties without WhatsApp's approval.
  • "Using these apps or linking your WhatsApp account to unofficial versions of WhatsApp violates our Terms of Service."
  • WhatsApp says it can't validate their security or privacy practices.
  • Your account "might also be temporarily or permanently banned, or it could lead to restrictions on your account, including the ability to link devices."

The examples it names are modified phone apps such as GB WhatsApp and FM WhatsApp, followed by "and others". The page does not name ZapFast. Its wording covers linking your account to any unofficial version, though, and ZapFast links as a device.

What ZapFast says about the risk

ZapFast doesn't hide this. Its README says it "is an unofficial client and is not affiliated with WhatsApp or Meta," that using it "may be against WhatsApp's terms of service and could get an account suspended," and "Use it at your own risk."

Its Account safety section adds that it sends normal read receipts and doesn't automate messages or send in bulk. It still says WhatsApp may restrict accounts that use unofficial clients, and to use an official client if you can't accept that risk.

So both sides agree: the code may be fine, and the account is still exposed.

If you've already been logged out or banned

WhatsApp says you might see a message like "You need the official WhatsApp to log in" or "You have been logged out for using an unofficial app." Its advice is to remove unofficial apps and go back to the official app.

For the full appeal steps, see our guide on what to do if your WhatsApp account is banned.

Official ways to use WhatsApp on a computer

If you are looking at ZapFast because WhatsApp feels heavy, the official options are WhatsApp Web in a browser and WhatsApp's desktop apps. ZapFast's site points out that WhatsApp has no official Linux app, so on Linux the official route is WhatsApp Web.

On Windows, our guide on WhatsApp for Windows using too much RAM covers why the app is heavy and how WhatsApp Web in a browser you already keep open compares. Both are official, so neither puts your account at the risk described above.

FAQ

Is ZapFast safe to use with WhatsApp?

ZapFast is open source and says it keeps an encrypted archive on your computer. The risk is to your account: WhatsApp's Help Center says linking your account to an unofficial version of WhatsApp violates its Terms of Service and can lead to a temporary or permanent ban. ZapFast's own README says the same.

Will WhatsApp ban me for using ZapFast?

Nobody can say for sure. WhatsApp says accounts using unofficial apps might be banned or restricted, including losing the ability to link devices. ZapFast says WhatsApp may restrict accounts that use unofficial clients.

Is ZapFast an official WhatsApp app?

No. Its README says it is an unofficial client and is not affiliated with WhatsApp or Meta.

Does ZapFast use less memory than WhatsApp Web?

ZapFast reports about 200 MB of idle RAM in its own Linux test, against 1.13 GB for WhatsApp Web with its Chromium processes. We have not measured it ourselves.

Is WA Folders an unofficial WhatsApp client?

No. WA Folders doesn't connect to WhatsApp on its own or link a device. It is a browser extension that works inside WhatsApp Web, the official web version, after you open it yourself.
