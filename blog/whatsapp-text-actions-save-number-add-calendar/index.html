Dotted underline in WhatsApp messages? How to save a number or add a date to your calendar

Published November 2, 2026

If a phone number or a date in one of your WhatsApp chats suddenly has a dotted line under it, that's a new feature. WABetaInfo reported on November 2, 2026 that WhatsApp is rolling out "message text actions" on Android and iPhone. The app spots four kinds of text in a message (phone numbers, dates and times, street addresses, and email addresses), marks them with a dotted underline, and gives you a short list of things to do with them when you tap.

It works on messages you receive and messages you send. Nothing changes if you ignore the underline, so you can keep chatting as before.

What each shortcut does

According to WABetaInfo's report, tapping the underlined text opens a small popup. The text WhatsApp detected is shown at the top, so you can check it picked up the whole number or the right date before you do anything. The options depend on what was detected:

  • Phone number: copy it, add it to your contacts, or call it through your phone carrier (a regular call, not a WhatsApp call). If the number belongs to a business, you can also see its business info.
  • Date or time: add it to your phone's calendar, copy it, or create a WhatsApp event from it.
  • Address: copy it or open it in your maps app.
  • Email address: open it in your mail app or copy it.

Some of these worked before in a rougher form, like long-pressing a number to copy it. The new part is the clear underline and one menu for all of them.

At the bottom of the popup WhatsApp asks whether the suggestion was useful. If you pick "not useful", you can say whether it grabbed the wrong text or you didn't need the action. WABetaInfo says this sends a flag only, not the message itself.

Is WhatsApp reading my messages to do this?

No. WABetaInfo reports that the detection runs on your phone using simple pattern matching, the same kind of rule that recognizes "a string of digits that looks like a phone number". Nothing is sent to WhatsApp's servers to make the suggestion, so end-to-end encryption isn't affected.

How to save a number from a chat to your phone, not only to WhatsApp

This is where people get caught out. When you tap Add to contacts, WhatsApp may save the person as a WhatsApp contact rather than in your phone's address book. WhatsApp's help page on contact upload says that when you add a new contact for the first time, the sync option is off by default, so the contact stays inside WhatsApp. The setting you choose sticks for future contacts until you change it.

That explains a long-running r/whatsapp thread where people save a number through WhatsApp and then can't find it in their phone's Contacts app. To avoid that:

  1. Tap the underlined number and choose Add to contacts.
  2. Fill in the name.
  3. Before saving, look for the Sync contact to phone switch and turn it on. On Android you may be asked which account to save to (phone storage or your Google account), so pick the one your phone contacts normally use.
  4. Save, then open your phone's Contacts app and check the person is there.

If you already saved someone and they only show up in WhatsApp, one fix that worked for people in that Reddit thread was opening the contact in WhatsApp, editing it, choosing to save it to the phone, and saving again. You can also find the overall setting in Settings > Privacy > Contacts, where WABetaInfo describes a WhatsApp Contacts option. Be careful there: WhatsApp's help page says turning off saving contacts to your WhatsApp account deletes the contacts saved to it, including ones synced from your address book. Contacts that exist only in WhatsApp would be gone, so copy any you need to your phone first.

Adding a date to your calendar, or making it a WhatsApp event

When someone writes "dentist Thursday 3pm" or "the 14th at 7", you now get two choices from the underline.

Add to calendar sends the date to your phone's calendar app. Use this for anything you want reminders for outside WhatsApp, or that other people in your life need to see on a shared calendar.

Create event makes a WhatsApp event inside the chat. Per WhatsApp's help page on events, an event has a name, date and time, and can include a description, end time, location and a WhatsApp call link. People in the chat can answer Going, Maybe or Not going, and those who say Going or Maybe get a notification when it starts. A few limits from the same page are worth knowing before you rely on it:

  • Only the person who created the event can edit or cancel it, and there's no co-host.
  • You can't forward an event to another chat or invite people who aren't in that chat.
  • People who join a group later can't see events created before they joined.
  • If disappearing messages is on, you may not be able to create events far in advance.

Whichever you pick, check the date in the popup first. Pattern matching can misread something like "3/4", which means different days in different countries.

I don't see the dotted underline

The feature is rolling out gradually. WABetaInfo says it's available to some users on the latest versions of WhatsApp for Android and iPhone, and that more accounts should get it over the coming weeks. WhatsApp hasn't announced a date for everyone.

Until it reaches you:

  1. Update WhatsApp from the Play Store or App Store. Having the latest version doesn't guarantee the feature, but you won't get it without it.
  2. Long-press the message and copy the text, then paste the number into your dialer or the date into your calendar by hand.
  3. To message a number without saving it, start a new chat and type the full number with the country code into the search box.

One more thing that looks similar but isn't the same feature: links and numbers from someone you've never talked to sometimes show up as plain text you can't tap. Users on r/whatsapp say they become tappable once you reply to the sender or add them to your contacts. If the sender is a stranger, that's a reason to slow down before tapping anything they sent.
