WhatsApp Close Friends for Status: how to set it up and who can still see it

Published November 18, 2026

WhatsApp Status now has a Close friends audience, the same idea as Close Friends on Instagram Stories. You put a few people on the list, and when you post a status you can send it to that list instead of all your contacts. WhatsApp's status privacy article for Android now lists Close friends next to the older options, and since November 17 people on X have been posting that it showed up in their app. Others are still looking for it.

This post covers how to use it, what the purple ring means, what to do if you don't have it yet, and the few cases where someone outside the list can still see a status.

How to share a status with Close friends

The list sits inside the normal status audience picker. WhatsApp's guide to sharing a status says to tap Status (Contacts) while you edit a status to choose who sees it.

  1. Open WhatsApp on your phone and go to Updates.
  2. Tap Add status and pick a photo or video, or write a text status.
  3. Before you send it, tap Status (Contacts) at the bottom.
  4. Choose Close friends.
  5. The first time, add the people you want on the list, then go back and send the status.

You don't have to build the list from scratch. WABetaInfo reported that WhatsApp creates the Close friends list for you and that you can add or remove people at any time. Menu names can differ a little between Android and iPhone, so look for the audience option on the screen where you send the status.

You can make more than one list

According to WABetaInfo, you can also create your own lists, each with a name and an emoji: family, a work team, a class. You start one with the New button in the status privacy screen, and you pick the list that fits each time you post.

Choose the name and emoji with care. WhatsApp's Help Center says people in Close friends see "the audience name and emoji" on your status. If you call a list "Not my in-laws", your in-laws won't see it, but everyone you did put on it will see that name.

What the purple ring means

People in your Close friends list see a purple ring around your status, the Help Center says. WABetaInfo reports the same purple ring for custom lists.

So if you see a purple ring on someone's picture, that person put you on one of their lists. You won't see who else is on it. WhatsApp says the audience "can't see who else is included".

The ring has already confused people. In a r/whatsapp thread from July, a user asked why one contact had a multicoloured ring and another a green one. The answer they found was that the multicoloured ring meant a Close friends status and the green one meant all contacts.

Do people know if you add or remove them?

No. WABetaInfo and BetaNews both report that WhatsApp sends no notification when you add someone to a list or take them off. People outside the list aren't told the status exists.

Someone who used to see your purple-ring statuses and then stops may still work it out on their own.

Changes only apply to new statuses

Editing the list doesn't change statuses you've already posted. WhatsApp's status privacy article says changes to your privacy settings "won't affect any status you've already shared", and WABetaInfo reports the same for lists. If you post to Close friends and then remove someone, they can keep seeing that status until it disappears, which WhatsApp says happens after 24 hours.

If a status went to the wrong people, delete it.

When people outside the list can still see it

These come from WhatsApp's own status privacy article and apply whatever audience you pick:

  • Mentions. If you mention a person or a group in a status, they can see it even if they aren't in the audience. Mention a group and every member of that group can see it.
  • Resharing by people you mention. They can reshare your status to their own audience. WhatsApp says the reshare shows the content but not your name, profile info or phone number.
  • Allow sharing. The Android article describes an Allow sharing switch that lets people who can see your status reshare and forward it. Keep it off for a Close friends status.
  • Group status. A status posted to a group is visible to everyone in the group, regardless of your status privacy settings.

Nothing stops someone on the list from photographing their screen with another phone. A list controls who sees the status, not what they do with it.

For a status meant for five people, don't mention anyone outside those five and don't post it to a group.

If you don't have Close friends yet

The feature reached beta testers on Android and iPhone in May, and WABetaInfo said it would reach more users over the following weeks. BetaNews noted that Meta hasn't given a timeline or a list of countries, so you and a friend may see different menus.

Update WhatsApp from the Play Store or App Store and check again. Until it shows up, the older setting does a similar job. In the status privacy screen, choose Only share with… and pick the contacts you want. WABetaInfo describes lists as a way to skip changing that setting each time you post, so expect more taps this way.

If the problem is one boss or one relative, My contacts except… is simpler: it hides your status from the people you pick and everyone else still sees it.

On WhatsApp Web and the desktop app

The list reports above cover the phone apps. WhatsApp's Web version of the status privacy article lists three options: My contacts, My contacts except… and Only share with…. If you post statuses from your computer and don't see Close friends there, use Only share with…, or post that status from your phone.
