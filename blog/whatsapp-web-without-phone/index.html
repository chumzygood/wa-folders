Does WhatsApp Web work without your phone? Yes, with two catches

Published November 22, 2026

Yes, WhatsApp Web keeps sending and receiving messages while your phone is off or has no internet, as long as you open WhatsApp on the phone at least once every 14 days, but it only ever holds part of your chat history, so older messages can still be missing.

People keep asking how this can work. If the phone is dead, where are the messages coming from? The short version is that WhatsApp Web stopped being a mirror of your phone a few years ago. It's now a "linked device" with its own connection. Everything below comes from WhatsApp's Help Center pages, read on November 22, 2026.

Why WhatsApp Web doesn't need your phone online

WhatsApp's About linked devices page says each linked device "connects to WhatsApp independently" and keeps the same end-to-end encryption. So your browser gets new messages straight from WhatsApp, not by way of your phone. Your phone can be switched off, in airplane mode or out of signal, and Web still works.

You can link up to four devices at a time: browsers, the Windows and Mac apps, tablets, a second phone, watches, some cars and AI glasses. The phone is still the main device in two ways. WhatsApp says you need it to register your account and to link each new device. You can't set up WhatsApp Web with only a computer.

Catch 1: the 14-day rule and the 30-day rule

Two different timers can log WhatsApp Web out, and they're easy to mix up.

  • 14 days without the phone. WhatsApp's How to link a device page says you need to log in to WhatsApp on your primary phone every 14 days to keep linked devices connected. A phone that sits switched off in a drawer for two weeks takes WhatsApp Web down with it.
  • 30 days without Web. The linked devices page also says WhatsApp automatically disconnects linked devices after 30 days of inactivity. A browser you haven't opened WhatsApp Web in for a month will show the QR code again.

If you travel and leave your phone at home, the 14-day rule is the one to plan around. Opening WhatsApp on the phone once before day 14 keeps everything linked.

Catch 2: Web never gets your full history

When you link WhatsApp Web, your phone sends it an encrypted copy of your most recent messages, and Web stores that copy in the browser. That's described on WhatsApp's message history page. The same page says "Not all messages and chats are synced to linked devices from your phone," that the Windows and Mac app syncs more history than WhatsApp Web, and that to see or search your full history you should check your phone.

So the phone has to be online when you link. After that, new messages reach Web directly. But anything older than what the phone handed over at linking time stays on the phone. If you're away from your phone and need a message from last year, WhatsApp Web may not have it, and there's no button on Web that fetches more from a phone that's offline.

On November 22, 2026, the How to link a device page also still carried this notice: "There is a known issue for some linked devices not displaying up to one year of chat history." WhatsApp says it's working on a fix and that your history is still visible on your primary device. Our guide to WhatsApp Web not showing older messages goes through what to try when days or months are missing.

What doesn't work on WhatsApp Web, phone or no phone

WhatsApp lists three things linked devices can't do right now:

  • view a live location
  • create or view broadcast lists
  • interact with businesses through WhatsApp Flows

Those need the phone whether it's online or not.

If your phone is lost or stolen

WhatsApp Web stays logged in until something logs it out, which is useful if your phone is gone and dangerous if someone else has it. Two facts from WhatsApp's account recovery page matter here:

  • Blocking the SIM does not log out other devices from your WhatsApp account.
  • When you re-register your number with the 6-digit code on a new phone, every device logged into your account is logged out automatically, WhatsApp Web included.

After you're back in, link your computer again with the QR code. If you get stuck on a code timer first, see what to do when WhatsApp says to wait before requesting a new code.

Getting older messages onto a computer

Since Web only has what the phone sent it, here are the options for older messages:

  1. Use the phone. WhatsApp's own answer for full history.
  2. Use the WhatsApp app for Windows or Mac. WhatsApp says it syncs more history than WhatsApp Web. On Windows it costs more memory, as covered in why WhatsApp for Windows uses so much RAM.
  3. Scroll up in the chat on Web. Web loads older messages as you scroll, until it reaches the end of its own copy.

FAQ

Can I use WhatsApp Web if my phone is off?

Yes. WhatsApp says each linked device connects to WhatsApp on its own, so WhatsApp Web sends and receives messages while your phone is off or offline. You still need to open WhatsApp on the phone at least once every 14 days to keep Web connected.

How long does WhatsApp Web stay logged in without my phone?

WhatsApp says you need to log in on your primary phone every 14 days to keep linked devices connected. Separately, it disconnects a linked device after 30 days of inactivity on that device.

Why can't WhatsApp Web load my older messages when my phone is offline?

WhatsApp Web only has the recent history your phone sent it when you linked it, plus messages since then. WhatsApp says not all messages and chats are synced to linked devices, so older ones stay on your phone and Web can't fetch them from a phone that's offline.

Can I set up WhatsApp Web without a phone?

No. WhatsApp says you need your primary device to register your account and to link each new device, either by scanning a QR code or by linking with your phone number.

Does WhatsApp Web log out if my phone is stolen?

Not by itself, and blocking the SIM doesn't log it out either. Re-registering your number with the 6-digit code on another phone logs out every linked device. You can also log out a device from Linked devices on your phone.
