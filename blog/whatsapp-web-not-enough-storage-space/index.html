WhatsApp Web says not enough storage space? Check the browser quota

Published November 26, 2026

When WhatsApp Web says there isn't enough storage space but your disk has plenty free, the limit is usually the storage quota your browser gives web.whatsapp.com, not your drive, so check that quota, clear only WhatsApp's site data, and use a normal window or a fresh browser profile.

Your chats stay on your phone through all of this. The steps below come from browser documentation and one public bug report we read on November 26, 2026, plus a quota check we ran ourselves the same day.

Why "free space" and "storage space" aren't the same thing

WhatsApp Web keeps a copy of your chats inside the browser, using the storage every website gets for its own data. Browsers don't hand a site your whole disk. They give each site (each origin, like web.whatsapp.com) a quota, and MDN's page on storage quotas lists how each browser sets it:

  • Chrome and Edge: a site can store up to 60% of the total disk size.
  • Firefox: the smaller of 10% of the disk or 10 GiB, for normal (best-effort) storage.
  • Safari (macOS 14 and later): around 60% of the total disk for each site.
  • Private windows (Incognito, InPrivate): browsers may use different quotas, and the data is usually deleted when the window closes.

MDN also notes that a site may never reach its quota, because the quota is worked out from the disk's total size, not the space that's actually free. When a site tries to store more than it's allowed, the browser refuses the write. WhatsApp hasn't published exactly what triggers its storage message, but those are the browser-side limits it runs into.

A real case: the browser was capping WhatsApp at about 320 MB

In a bug report on the FireDragon browser (a Firefox-based browser), the person who opened it said WhatsApp Web crashed 5 to 10 minutes after opening with a "not enough storage space" message, then logged them out. In 2025 another FireDragon user with the same problem added that clearing cookies and site data didn't help, and that storage stopped at about 300 MB. When they compared storage estimates on the same computer, FireDragon gave web.whatsapp.com a quota of 335,652,454 bytes (about 320 MiB), while another Firefox-based browser, Floorp, gave it 10,737,418,240 bytes (10 GiB). In a fresh FireDragon profile, WhatsApp's storage grew to about 1.3 GB. After they signed in to Firefox Sync in that new profile, WhatsApp stopped working again.

So the disk wasn't full. Something in that browser profile was holding WhatsApp to a small quota.

What we measured

On November 26, 2026 we opened web.whatsapp.com in Google Chrome 156 with a fresh profile, on a Linux test computer with about 112 GiB free, and asked the browser for its storage estimate. Chrome reported a quota of 10,737,418,240 bytes (10 GiB) for the site and 0 bytes used, because nothing was linked yet. That's far less than the free space on the disk, and it's still plenty for most accounts. The number your browser gives will depend on the browser, the profile and the disk, which is why it's worth checking.

How to check the quota your browser gives WhatsApp Web

  1. Open web.whatsapp.com in the browser that shows the error.
  2. Open the developer tools (F12, or Ctrl+Shift+I; Cmd+Option+I on a Mac) and click Console.
  3. Type await navigator.storage.estimate() and press Enter.

The result shows quota and usage in bytes. MDN calls these estimates, so treat them as rough. If the quota is in the hundreds of millions of bytes (a few hundred MB) while your drive has tens of gigabytes free, the browser or the profile is the limit.

Fixes, in order

1. Use a normal window

If you opened WhatsApp Web in Incognito or a private window, switch to a normal one. Private windows can get a different quota and lose their data when they close, so WhatsApp has to download your chats again every time.

2. Clear only WhatsApp Web's site data

This removes the copy in the browser, not your chats. In Chrome, open web.whatsapp.com, click the icon to the left of the address, choose Site settings and delete the site's data. Chrome Help covers clearing data for one site. Then link the computer again with the QR code. WhatsApp Help also suggests clearing the browser's cache and cookies and logging back in when WhatsApp Web won't connect. Our database error guide walks through the same clean-up step by step.

3. Check the drive the browser lives on

The browser stores its data on the drive where your profile is, usually the system drive (C: on Windows). If you have a big second drive with lots of space but the system drive is nearly full, that's the one that counts. MDN says browsers start deleting stored site data when the device runs low on space, starting with the sites used least recently.

4. Try a fresh profile or another browser

In the FireDragon case, a new profile fixed it until a sync setting came back. Make a new profile (in Chrome, click your profile picture and choose Add), or open WhatsApp Web in a different up-to-date browser. If it works there, the old profile is the problem: a setting, a privacy extension or a browser fork that limits site storage.

5. Remove old linked sessions from your phone

On your phone, open Linked devices, remove the stuck computer and link it again. That starts a clean sync for that browser.

FAQ

Why does WhatsApp Web say there's not enough storage when my computer has space?

Because the browser gives each website its own storage quota, and WhatsApp Web keeps its copy of your chats inside that quota. The quota is set by the browser and the profile, not by the free space on your disk, so a profile that caps sites at a few hundred megabytes can trigger the message on a nearly empty drive.

Will clearing WhatsApp Web's site data delete my chats?

No. It deletes only the copy stored in that browser. Your chats stay on your phone, and WhatsApp Web downloads a fresh copy when you link the computer again with the QR code.

How can I see how much storage my browser gives WhatsApp Web?

Open web.whatsapp.com, open the developer tools console (F12), type await navigator.storage.estimate() and press Enter. It returns an estimated quota and usage in bytes.

Does Incognito or a private window make it worse?

It can. MDN says private windows may get different quotas, and their stored data is usually deleted when the window closes, so WhatsApp has to sync again each time. Use a normal window.

Can a WhatsApp Web extension fix the storage error?

Not WA Folders. It doesn't change the browser's storage quota. Turn extensions off while you test a fresh profile or another browser, so you can tell what fixed it.
