WhatsApp stickers with sound: how to make one (and why it's missing)

Published November 28, 2026

Since November 25, some WhatsApp users can send stickers that play a sound when the other person taps them. You make one from a short video on your phone, then keep the video's own audio, pick a sound effect, import a track or record your voice. If you don't see the option yet, that's expected. It is reaching beta testers first, and WhatsApp hasn't said when everyone gets it (The Times of India).

This guide is based on reports from WABetaInfo, GSMArena and The Times of India, read on November 28, 2026. We didn't find a WhatsApp Help Center page for sound stickers that day, so menu names may change before the full release.

Who has sound stickers right now

According to WABetaInfo, the feature arrived with WhatsApp beta for Android 2.26.47.1 from the Google Play Store, and only some beta testers have it. It first showed up, unfinished, in the 2.26.41.3 beta in October.

On iPhone, GSMArena says it is reaching some TestFlight testers and some App Store users. Digital Trends names iOS beta 26.46.10.70.

None of these reports mention WhatsApp Web or the desktop app, so plan to make sound stickers on your phone.

How to make a sound sticker

These steps follow WABetaInfo's walkthrough for Android:

  1. Save the video you want to use to your phone's gallery. Keep it short (see the length section below).
  2. Open any chat and open the sticker keyboard.
  3. Tap Create sticker.
  4. Pick the video. WhatsApp turns it into an animated sticker on its own.
  5. In the editor, tap the new audio button at the top. This opens an Add sound screen.
  6. Choose where the sound comes from (the four options are below), then send the sticker.

To keep it with your other custom stickers, WhatsApp's sticker help page explains how to group custom stickers into a pack: tap and hold the sticker, choose Add to sticker pack, and pick a pack or tap New.

The four ways to add audio

The Add sound screen gives you these choices (WABetaInfo, GSMArena):

  • Original audio. Keep the sound already in the video. This is the one to use for a meme clip where the noise is the joke.
  • Preset effect. Pick from a built-in library. Digital Trends lists effects such as Cat Purr, Crickets and Weird Kid Laugh in the iOS beta.
  • Import a file. Use an audio track saved in your phone's files.
  • Record a voice note. Say something yourself and attach it to the animation.

How a sound sticker plays for the other person

The sticker arrives with a small audio icon on it, so people can tell it has sound. It stays muted until someone taps that icon. When they do, the audio plays three times in a loop and stops (WABetaInfo). A sound sticker won't start blaring in a family group on its own.

WABetaInfo also reports that the sticker and its audio are end-to-end encrypted, like your other messages.

How long should the clip be?

None of the reports give a length limit for sound stickers. Its sticker help page does list rules for animated stickers you upload in a pack: no more than 10 seconds of animation and up to 500 KB per sticker. That's a reasonable target. Trim the clip to the part you want before you start.

No audio button? Check these

  • Update WhatsApp. On Android you need at least beta 2.26.47.1. If you aren't a tester, see how to join the WhatsApp beta.
  • Wait a little. Digital Trends notes that even on the right build, the button may not appear right away, because WhatsApp switches it on for accounts gradually.
  • Start from a video, not a photo. The reports describe the audio button only in the editor you get after picking a video.
  • Check where you're looking. The reports cover the Android and iPhone apps. On a computer, WhatsApp Web has its own photo sticker maker, and none of the sources say it can add sound.

What about sticker bots?

Some services offer to turn a clip or a link into a sound sticker if you message it to them on WhatsApp. That can work, but it means handing your video, and your phone number, to a service you know little about. Now that WhatsApp's own editor can do this, try that first. If it hasn't reached you yet, waiting a few weeks is the safer choice.
