Removed from the WhatsApp beta? How to rejoin on iPhone and Android

Published October 25, 2026

If WhatsApp beta disappeared from your iPhone's TestFlight app this week, you're not the only one. On October 24, WABetaInfo, the site that tracks WhatsApp test builds, posted that WhatsApp had removed many users from the iOS beta program and that it would share a new TestFlight invite link once one becomes available. No reason was given for the removals.

The timing stung, because the iPhone beta had just started getting scheduled messages. WhatsApp began rolling that feature out to some iOS testers on build 26.42.10.19 a few days earlier.

Below is how each beta program works right now, what you can do to get back in, and the scam to watch out for while people are scrambling for a spot.

Watch out for people selling invite links

In the same post, WABetaInfo warned people not to trust anyone selling invite links or invitation codes for the WhatsApp beta, and called it a scam. The iPhone beta uses a public TestFlight link that costs nothing. When there's room, anyone with the link can join. When it's full, nobody can get you in, no matter what they charge.

If someone in a group chat or a Telegram channel offers you a "private" beta code, skip it. The best case is that you lose the money. The worst case is that the link leads to a fake page asking for your Apple ID or your WhatsApp verification code.

Rejoining the WhatsApp beta on iPhone

Apple's TestFlight runs the iOS beta, and WhatsApp limits how many testers it takes. WABetaInfo's TestFlight page says that in October 2026 the program is not available to new testers. New spots open when current testers leave or when WhatsApp expands the program. WABetaInfo says it monitors the program and posts an alert on its WhatsApp channel when spots open.

When a spot does open:

  1. Install TestFlight from the App Store on the iPhone you want to test on.
  2. Open the public invite link on that iPhone. WABetaInfo links to it from its TestFlight page once the program is open again.
  3. Tap Accept, then Install. If you're a returning tester and WhatsApp still shows in TestFlight, tap Update or Open instead.

A few details from the same page explain why the app might vanish even if you didn't get kicked out. Each beta build can be tested for up to 90 days after WhatsApp uploads it, and TestFlight shows how many days are left under the app name. If you stop updating, the build you have can expire. Turning on automatic updates for WhatsApp inside TestFlight avoids that.

If you've given up on the beta for now, open TestFlight, go to the WhatsApp page, tap Stop Testing, and then install WhatsApp from the App Store. Make an iCloud backup first in WhatsApp under Settings, Chats, Chat Backup, so your chats are safe if the switch asks you to restore.

Joining the WhatsApp beta on Android

On Android, the beta runs through Google Play's testing program, and it has been full for a long time. A thread on r/whatsapp from October 2025 is typical: someone who had been trying for over a year kept getting a message saying WhatsApp couldn't accept new testers, and the top reply said the beta was full.

The full message reads roughly like this: the WhatsApp Messenger testing program "has reached the maximum number of testers" and "isn't accepting any more testers." Spots do free up, though, and people do get in. Here's the routine:

  1. Open the WhatsApp testing page on Google Play in a browser: play.google.com/apps/testing/com.whatsapp
  2. Sign in with the same Google account your phone uses for the Play Store.
  3. If you see Become a tester, tap it. Within a few minutes, the Play Store should offer an update and the app becomes WhatsApp Messenger (beta).
  4. If it says the program is full, come back and reload the page later. Android Police's guide says this is the normal route and that you can eventually get through by refreshing.

You can also check the bottom of the WhatsApp listing in the Play Store app for a Join the beta section. If it's missing or says the beta is full, use the browser page above instead.

There is a third option some people use: installing beta APK files from a mirror site such as APKMirror, which hosts beta versions of Play Store apps. You don't need a tester spot for that, but you're installing files from outside the Play Store and you won't get automatic beta updates, so treat it as a last resort.

Leaving the Android beta

To go back to the regular app, open the WhatsApp listing in the Play Store, scroll to "You're a beta tester," and tap Leave. Google Play may warn that the program has limited space and that you might not get back in. The stable version won't arrive as a normal update, according to Android Police; you have to uninstall and reinstall WhatsApp. Run a Google Drive backup first under Settings, Chats, Chat backup, and make sure it finished before you uninstall.

Is the beta worth the trouble?

That depends on what you want from it. Beta builds get new features first, but WhatsApp rolls most features out to a share of testers at a time, so joining doesn't guarantee you'll see the thing you joined for. Scheduled messages, for example, are reaching some beta testers on Android and iPhone while others on the same build still don't have them.

Betas also break things. If WhatsApp is how you run a business or keep in touch with family, an unstable build at the wrong moment costs you more than waiting a few weeks for the feature to reach the regular app. If you only want to know what's coming, following WABetaInfo's posts tells you as much as the beta does, without the bugs.
