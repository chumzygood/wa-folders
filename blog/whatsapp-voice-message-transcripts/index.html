How to turn on WhatsApp voice message transcripts

Published October 14, 2026

Long voice notes are fine when you have headphones and a quiet minute. They are less fine on a crowded bus, in an open office, or when someone left you a three-minute update you only need the punchline from.

WhatsApp can turn those voice messages into text on your phone. The catch: the feature ships turned off, language support still differs by platform, and WhatsApp Web does not include it. A dpa write-up republished on Yahoo Tech on October 14, 2026 walked through the same setup steps many people are still discovering.

What transcripts actually do

According to WhatsApp's Help Center:

  • Transcripts are text versions of voice messages you receive.
  • They are generated on your device. WhatsApp says no one outside the chat, including WhatsApp, can listen to the audio or read the transcript.
  • Your chats stay end-to-end encrypted.
  • Only you (the recipient) see the transcript. The sender does not.
  • Transcripts can be wrong. WhatsApp says so in the Help Center; noisy audio and accents are common failure points.

You still get the original voice message. Transcription is optional reading, not a replacement for the audio.

Turn it on on Android

WhatsApp's Android Help Center steps:

  1. Open WhatsApp Settings.
  2. Tap Chats.
  3. Turn Voice Message Transcripts on.
  4. Tap Choose language and pick the language you receive most voice messages in.
  5. Tap Set up now or Wait for Wi-Fi so the language pack can download.

To change the language later: Settings → Chats → Transcript language. Turning transcripts off resets that language choice.

To read a message: tap and hold the voice message, open the menu, tap Transcribe, then expand the text in the bubble if only a preview shows. It can take a moment.

Android languages right now

The Help Center currently lists four languages for Android: English, Portuguese, Spanish and Russian.

WABetaInfo has also reported Android beta builds that add more packs (including Arabic and Hindi, plus more variants for English, German, French, Italian and Japanese). Those packs are rolling out gradually. If a language you need is missing in Settings, check for a WhatsApp update and look again later. Do not assume a beta language is on every account yet.

Turn it on on iPhone

WhatsApp requires iOS 16 or newer and Siri enabled. Yahoo's Oct 14 write-up repeats the same requirements.

  1. Tap WhatsApp Settings or your profile picture.
  2. Tap Chats.
  3. Turn Voice Message Transcripts on.
  4. Tap Choose language and select the language you hear most often.
  5. Confirm with Change to {language} (the button names the language you picked).

If Siri was off when you enabled transcripts, download the selected language when WhatsApp asks. Without Siri, you can hit "Transcript unavailable."

To read a message: tap and hold the voice note, tap Transcribe, then expand the text if needed.

iPhone languages

WhatsApp's iOS Help Center splits the list by system version:

  • iOS 16+: English, Arabic, Chinese, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish and Turkish.
  • iOS 17+: those languages plus Danish, Dutch, Finnish, Hebrew, Malay, Norwegian, Swedish and Thai.

Pick the language of the messages you receive, not your phone's display language, if those differ.

If Transcribe is missing or fails

Work through these before you assume the feature is gone:

  1. Update WhatsApp from the Play Store or App Store.
  2. Confirm transcripts are on under Settings → Chats.
  3. Confirm the transcript language matches the voice note. A Spanish message will not transcribe cleanly if your pack is English-only.
  4. On iPhone, confirm iOS 16+ and Siri are available.
  5. Wait for the language pack to finish downloading (especially if you chose Wait for Wi-Fi).
  6. Give the first transcript a minute. The Help Center says it can take a moment.

If the option never appears after an update and a restart, the account may not have the feature yet. WhatsApp has rolled transcripts out in waves since the November 2024 launch post on the WhatsApp Blog.

What phone transcripts do not cover

  • WhatsApp Web and desktop do not get the same built-in Transcribe control described above. The Help Center pages for this feature are phone-focused (Android and iOS).
  • One language pack at a time is the practical model: you choose the language you receive most. Mixed-language group chats are awkward.
  • Accuracy is not perfect. Treat the text as a skim aid, then play the audio when a name, number or deadline matters.
  • Senders never see that you transcribed. That also means you cannot rely on transcripts as a shared record inside the chat.

Searching and exporting later

Phone transcripts help you read one message in place. They do not, by themselves, give you a searchable archive of every voice note across chats the way typed messages are searchable.

If you work mostly on a computer, that gap shows up fast: you open WhatsApp Web, get a long voice note, and have no Transcribe button. For that case, a Chrome extension that runs transcription on your machine can put the text where search and chat export already look. Only reach for that if you need Web-side search or export; the phone feature is enough for reading in the mobile app.
