Is WhatsApp Web safe on a work computer? What India's new advisory says

Published November 23, 2026

Yes, for ordinary chats, if the computer is yours and you control who sits at it. The risk is the session staying open. Anyone who gets to your logged-in WhatsApp Web can read your chats and send messages as you, and closing the tab usually doesn't stop that. If you use WhatsApp Web at the office, untick Stay logged in on this browser before you scan the QR code, set an app lock, and check Linked devices on your phone now and then.

What India's advisory says

On November 18, 2026, the National Cybercrime Threat Analytics Unit (NCTAU) of the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), part of India's Ministry of Home Affairs, put out an advisory on the safe use of WhatsApp Web. ABP Live and The Economic Times reported it on November 23. According to those reports, the advisory:

  • warns that a logged-in WhatsApp Web session can be misused to read messages, send messages pretending to be you, and reach your contacts and groups, which can then be used for fraud and phishing;
  • asks people to be careful on office computers, shared systems, public computers and any device other people can get to;
  • says government, organisational, financial and confidential information should preferably not be exchanged through WhatsApp Web, and that organisations with sensitive work should avoid it on office systems;
  • says closing the browser window or tab is not the same as logging out, and asks people to log out by hand after every session;
  • treats Incognito or Private Browsing as an extra precaution only, for when you have to use a trusted computer briefly;
  • says not to log in at cyber cafes, public kiosks, hotel business centres or on borrowed computers.

The reports don't describe a new bug in WhatsApp. The advice is about sessions left open on machines other people can use.

Why an open session matters

WhatsApp Web is a linked device. WhatsApp's Help Center says each linked device connects independently and your messages stay end-to-end encrypted. Encryption protects messages on the way. It doesn't help if someone sits down at a computer where your chats are already open, or uses a browser profile where you are still signed in.

WhatsApp also says it can't tell you who accessed your account, or when and where. Your Linked devices list on the phone is the only check you get.

How to use WhatsApp Web on an office computer with less risk

1. Untick "Stay logged in" before you scan the code. On the QR code screen there's a Stay logged in on this browser option. WhatsApp's Help Center says that if you uncheck it, your session logs out automatically when you close the tab or browser. On a shared or office PC, this is the setting to change first.

2. Log out when you're done. If you did stay logged in, use Log out in WhatsApp Web's menu before you leave for the day. Closing the window isn't enough.

3. Turn on app lock. In WhatsApp Web, go to Settings > Privacy > App lock and set a password of 6 to 128 characters. You can have it lock itself after 1 minute, 15 minutes, 1 hour or 8 hours, and lock it by hand from the menu with Lock app. Per the Help Center, notifications don't appear while it's locked. Our guide on locking WhatsApp Web with a password has the full steps.

4. Lock the computer when you stand up. Press Windows key + L on Windows (Microsoft's shortcut list) or Control-Command-Q on a Mac (Apple's list). This protects everything on the screen, WhatsApp included.

5. Check Linked devices on your phone. On Android, tap the three-dot menu, then Linked devices. On iPhone, open Settings, then Linked devices. Tap any computer you don't recognise or no longer use and choose Log out. WhatsApp also disconnects linked devices after 30 days of inactivity, but don't wait for that. If you left a session open somewhere, our post on logging out a hotel or shared PC from your phone walks through it.

6. Keep the browser and the computer clean. The advisory also asks people to keep the operating system, browser and security software updated, and to avoid suspicious downloads, unknown files and cracked software. Only add browser extensions you trust to the browser you use for WhatsApp. If you think the computer is already infected, see what to do about WhatsApp Web malware and unknown linked devices.

Signs someone else is using your account

The advisory lists a few warning signs: messages in your sent history you didn't write, contacts replying to things you never said, and other activity you can't explain. If you see any of these, open Linked devices on your phone and log out every computer you don't need. The same Help Center page suggests turning on two-step verification for an extra layer.

Should you stop using WhatsApp Web at work?

That depends on what you send and whose computer it is. If your employer, or a government office, has told you not to use it for work, follow that. For confidential files, client money details or anything you'd be in trouble for leaking, use whatever channel your organisation approves.

For everyday chats on a computer only you use, the steps above cover most of what the advisory worries about. On a computer other people use, or one you don't control, the advisory's advice is the simplest version: don't log in there.
