Using WhatsApp abroad without losing your number (and the new travel eSIM)

Published November 27, 2026

WhatsApp is tied to the phone number you registered, not to the SIM card in your phone. You can put in a local SIM, add a travel eSIM or live on hotel Wi-Fi, and your chats, groups and contacts keep working on your usual number. The one thing that can go wrong is being asked to verify again while you're away, because that code goes to your home number. Most of this guide is about avoiding that, and about what to do if it happens anyway.

WhatsApp also said on November 26, 2026 that it will soon sell travel data eSIMs inside the app. What we know about that is further down. Everything else here comes from WhatsApp's Help Center, read on November 27, 2026.

Why a new SIM doesn't change your WhatsApp

WhatsApp's page on phone numbers and SIMs says your account is linked to the number you verified, not to a particular SIM card or SIM slot. Removing or swapping a SIM or eSIM does not sign you out. On a dual-SIM phone, WhatsApp uses only the verified number, and the second line has no effect on your account.

The same page adds the catch: to keep access, you need an active SIM or eSIM with your registered number. Day to day you won't notice. It matters the moment WhatsApp asks for a 6-digit code, which it sends by SMS or phone call to your home number. If that SIM is in a drawer at home, you can't receive it.

Before you leave

  1. Add and verify an email address. Go to Settings > Account > Email address. According to WhatsApp's email verification page, when you log back in to an existing account you may be able to get the code by email instead, and that route doesn't need an active SIM. It only works if the email was added and verified before you got logged out, and it isn't offered for first-time registration.
  2. Know your two-step verification PIN or password, if you turned it on. WhatsApp shows that screen right after you register your number, so a re-verification abroad will ask for it.
  3. Keep your home SIM with you. If your phone has two SIM slots or eSIM support, leave the home line installed alongside the travel one. Ask your carrier whether that line receives texts while abroad and what it costs.
  4. Stop media downloading on roaming data. On Android: Settings > Storage and data > Media auto-download > When roaming, then untick everything. WhatsApp's auto-download page warns that roaming data is often expensive. On iPhone the same menu lets you set each media type to download on Wi-Fi only (iPhone steps).
  5. If you'll use WhatsApp Web on a laptop, open WhatsApp on your phone at least once every 14 days. WhatsApp's linking page says linked devices stay connected only if you do. Our post on using WhatsApp Web without your phone covers the details.

While you're away: what not to tap

  • Don't log out, and don't delete and reinstall WhatsApp. WhatsApp's porting page says those are the cases where you have to verify your number again. A SIM change on its own doesn't trigger it.
  • Don't use Change number for the local SIM. Change number moves your account to a different phone number (SIM page). You don't need it to use a local SIM's data.
  • Don't keep requesting codes. WhatsApp makes you wait longer after each request, and asking again too soon can extend the wait (porting page). If you're already stuck on a timer, see why WhatsApp makes you wait before requesting a new code.
  • Don't hop between phones. The SIM page warns that switching your account between devices too often can block you from verifying it again.

Getting data abroad

Any internet connection works: Wi-Fi, roaming on your home plan, a local SIM or a travel eSIM. The difference is price and convenience. The help page of one eSIM seller, Orange Travel, points out that a data-only plan is enough for WhatsApp, that WhatsApp calls use data rather than call minutes, and that video calls use much more data than messages. Size the plan around how many video calls you expect to make.

WhatsApp's travel eSIM: what's confirmed so far

WhatsApp's announcement says you'll be able to buy and install a data eSIM for international trips without leaving the app, then keep using WhatsApp on your normal number when you land. Partners named so far are Jio, Smart Axiata, Smart Communications and Verizon, plus others. It will roll out in select countries "soon", and the option appears in the app once it reaches you. There is nothing to switch on yet.

Not announced yet, per AndroidPure and Beebom: prices, plan sizes, the list of countries and a launch date. WhatsApp calls it a data eSIM, so Beebom notes that calling doesn't appear to be included.

Some details come from test versions of the app and could change before launch:

  • Android Authority found travel eSIM screens in WhatsApp beta 2.26.47.10, with signs of destination search, fixed-data and unlimited plans, and warnings when data runs low or a plan is about to end.
  • Beebom, citing WABetaInfo, reports the option will sit on the Account screen, and that the eSIM switches on by itself when the phone connects to a network at your destination.
  • Strings in the beta code suggest you'll need an eSIM-capable, unlocked phone, according to AndroidPure. WhatsApp's post doesn't mention device requirements, so check your phone before counting on it.

Beebom also points out that a data-only eSIM sits next to your existing SIM instead of replacing it. That matters in India, where WhatsApp has been building checks that your registered SIM is in the phone (our SIM binding post explains them).

When the option shows up, compare its price with your carrier's roaming pass and with standalone travel eSIM apps before you buy. Buying inside WhatsApp is quicker, but that says nothing about whether it's the cheapest data for your trip.

If WhatsApp logs you out mid-trip

  1. Get on a solid connection, then start logging back in. If you added an email before the trip, pick Email under Choose how to verify and enter the 6-digit code from your inbox.
  2. No email on the account? The code has to reach your home number by SMS or phone call. Put the home SIM back in, or switch its line on, and make sure it can receive texts or calls on roaming. Call me is offered once any wait timer ends.
  3. If no code arrives at all, work through the porting page's checklist: the number is active, the phone has signal and can receive texts and calls, and you typed the full number with the country code.

If you can't receive a code on the home number and never added an email, WhatsApp's email verification page says to ask your mobile provider for a replacement SIM with the same number.
