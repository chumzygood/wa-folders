How to add a cover photo to your WhatsApp profile, and who can see it

Published October 31, 2026

If you've opened your WhatsApp profile and wondered whether you can put a picture in the strip behind your profile photo, the answer used to be no. Back in June, someone asked exactly that on r/whatsapp, and the replies said the pink pattern was decoration and that banners were only for WhatsApp Business accounts.

That's changing. On October 30, 2026, WABetaInfo reported that WhatsApp is rolling out cover photos for personal profiles to some Android beta testers, and on October 31 it reported the same feature on the iPhone beta. A cover photo is a wide landscape image that sits at the top of your profile, above your round profile picture. You keep both.

This guide covers how to set one, how to control who sees it, and what to do if the option isn't there.

Who has it right now

Only some beta testers, and not all of them. According to WABetaInfo, the feature showed up in WhatsApp beta for Android 2.26.43.11 (Google Play) and WhatsApp beta for iOS 26.43.10.70 (TestFlight). Installing those versions doesn't guarantee you'll see it, because WhatsApp turns it on account by account. WABetaInfo says it should reach more accounts over the coming weeks, and WhatsApp hasn't given a date for the regular app.

So if you're on the normal version of WhatsApp from the Play Store or App Store, you won't find the setting yet. That isn't a bug on your phone.

If you want to try the beta anyway, our guide on how to join the WhatsApp beta walks through it for both platforms. The iPhone TestFlight program fills up often, so a spot isn't guaranteed.

How to add a cover photo

The steps are the same on Android and iPhone, according to WABetaInfo's reports on both builds.

  1. Open WhatsApp and go to your profile. On Android that's Settings, then tap your name or photo at the top. On iPhone, tap Settings at the bottom, then your name.
  2. Tap the Edit button on your profile page.
  3. Choose where the image comes from. You get three choices: take a new photo with the camera, pick one from your gallery or photo library, or generate one with Meta AI.
  4. WhatsApp crops the picture to a wide landscape shape. Check the preview, then save.

Because of that crop, pick an image that still makes sense as a thin horizontal strip. A wide landscape shot, a skyline or a plain texture works better than a tall portrait, where the crop can cut off a face or the part you care about.

If you don't set one, WhatsApp shows a default image in that spot.

Where your cover photo shows up

You'll see your own cover photo at the top of your profile page and at the top of the Settings screen.

Other people see it when they open your chat and tap your name to view the contact info screen. The banner appears above your profile picture, name and phone number. If your privacy setting doesn't allow someone to see it, WhatsApp doesn't show it to them.

How to control who sees it

The cover photo has its own privacy setting, separate from your profile picture. WABetaInfo says it lives on the same privacy screen as WhatsApp's other visibility options, so look under Settings, then Privacy.

There are four choices:

  • Everyone: any WhatsApp user who opens your contact info can see it. This is the default.
  • My contacts: only people saved in your phone's contacts.
  • My contacts except...: your contacts, minus the specific people you pick.
  • Nobody: the cover photo stays on your profile but no one else sees it.

Since the default is Everyone, anyone with your number can see the banner once you set one, including strangers in large groups. If you wouldn't post that picture publicly, switch the setting to My contacts before or right after you add it.

It's also worth matching this setting with your profile picture setting. If your profile photo is set to My contacts but your cover photo is set to Everyone, people outside your contacts will see the banner even though they can't see your face.

What to check before you use one

A cover photo is easy to forget about once it's set. A few things to think about:

  • Location clues. A photo of your street, your house or your office building tells people more than you might mean to share. That matters more with the Everyone setting.
  • Other people in the photo. If a friend or your kids are in the picture, they become part of a profile that anyone with your number can see.
  • Work contacts. If you use one WhatsApp account for both work and personal chats, clients will see the same banner your friends do. The "My contacts except" option lets you hide it from specific people.
  • Meta AI images. Generating a banner with Meta AI is optional. If you'd rather not use the AI feature, take a photo or pick one from your gallery instead.

Removing or changing it

WABetaInfo's reports describe editing the cover photo from the same Edit button on your profile page. If you want it gone from other people's view without deleting it, set the privacy option to Nobody.

If the option never appears

  • Update WhatsApp from the Play Store or App Store and check your profile again.
  • If you're on the beta, make sure you have at least the versions listed above.
  • Give it time. Staged rollouts like this one can take weeks to reach every account, and WhatsApp sometimes pauses or changes a feature during testing before it ships to everyone.
  • There's no setting, flag or third-party app that turns it on early. Modified versions of WhatsApp that promise extra features can get your account banned, so don't install one to get a banner.

Once WhatsApp releases cover photos in the regular app, the steps above should stay the same, though button names can shift between the beta and the final version.
