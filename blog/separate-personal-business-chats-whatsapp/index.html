Keep work and personal WhatsApp chats apart without a second number

Published November 5, 2026

You can keep work and family chats apart on one WhatsApp number by putting them into custom Lists on your phone, and on WhatsApp Web, where WhatsApp says Lists aren't available, a folder extension can do the same job. A true split, with separate profiles and notifications, needs a second account, and WhatsApp says that needs a second phone number.

I build WA Folders, one of the options below, so weigh that part with that in mind. Everything about WhatsApp itself here comes from WhatsApp's own Help Center and blog, which I checked on November 5, 2026.

Option 1: Lists on your phone (same number)

WhatsApp's About lists page calls Lists "customizable filters that can help you keep your chats organized." Every account starts with four: All, Unread, Favorites and Groups. You can add your own, such as Work and Family.

To make one on Android or iPhone:

  1. Open Chats and swipe down to see your lists.
  2. Tap +, name the list (for example, Work), then tap Add people or groups.
  3. Pick the chats and confirm.

To move a chat into a list later, tap and hold the chat, then choose Add to list. WhatsApp notes that you need at least one chat to create a list.

A list is a filter. Every chat still sits in All, and a work message still rings like any other. If you want a list to stay quiet, the mute guide covers muting a whole list on your phone, including the catch that @mentions still notify you.

Where Lists stop: WhatsApp Web

Open WhatsApp's How to use lists page with the web tab selected and it says: "This feature isn't available on the web app, but it is available on these devices," then names only Android and iOS. Your WhatsApp Web may still show a filter row above the chats, and which filters appear depends on your account, so check your own before deciding. The labels guide goes deeper on what Business accounts get on the web.

Option 2: A second account (needs a second number)

WhatsApp lets one phone run two accounts at once. Its help page says you need "a separate phone number" to register the second one, and the WhatsApp blog adds that this means a second SIM, or a phone that takes multi-SIM or eSIM.

Setup on the phone: open Settings, tap the arrow next to your name, then Add Account, and verify the second number with its 6-digit code. Each account gets its own photo, about text, notification settings and privacy settings. You still get alerts on the idle account and switch over to read them.

Three limits from that same page matter if you work at a computer:

  • The feature supports a maximum of two accounts.
  • Secondary accounts can only be created from your primary mobile device.
  • Only mobile companion devices can switch between accounts. WhatsApp Web can be linked to the second account, but it won't switch between the two.

Account switching also isn't available in the WhatsApp Business app, according to the same page.

Option 3: Folder tabs on WhatsApp Web (same number)

If you do most of your messaging on a computer and want one number, a Chrome extension can split the chat list for you. WA Folders runs only on web.whatsapp.com. On first use its Get started card offers Family, Work or Clients as a first folder. Open a chat, press Add chat, and the folder shows as a tab above the chat list. Click Work to see only work chats; click All to get WhatsApp's list back unchanged.

What I'd point out from building it:

  • A chat can sit in more than one folder, for a client who is also a friend.
  • Each tab carries unread, reply and question markers, so you can see whether Work or Family needs you before opening it.
  • Auto-file rules can put new chats in a folder by name, for example a name containing "Ltd" goes to Clients.
  • Folders live in Chrome on your computer. They don't show on your phone and don't change your WhatsApp Lists.
  • It never sends or types a message for you.

The WhatsApp Web folders page explains how the tabs and markers work, and the organizing guide walks through filing a long existing chat list.

Which one fits

  • Mostly on your phone, one number: use Lists, and mute the Work list after hours.
  • You need separate profiles, privacy or a work number you can hand out: get a second number and add a second account.
  • Mostly on WhatsApp Web, one number: Lists aren't available there, so a folder extension fills that gap. You can still keep Lists on your phone alongside it.

Once the chats are split, a different chat theme on WhatsApp Web for the work group makes it easy to tell which chat is open before you send.

FAQ

Can I have two WhatsApp accounts on one number?

No. WhatsApp's help page says a second account needs a separate phone number.

Do WhatsApp Lists work on WhatsApp Web?

WhatsApp's How to use lists page says the feature isn't available on the web app and lists only Android and iOS.

Does adding a chat to a Work list hide it from my main chat list?

No. A list is a filter. The chat still appears under All.

Can WhatsApp Web switch between my two accounts?

No. WhatsApp says only mobile companion devices can switch between accounts.

Do WA Folders folders sync to my phone?

No. They live in Chrome on your computer and don't change WhatsApp's own Lists.
