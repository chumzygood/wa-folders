How WhatsApp guest calling works (join a call without an account)

Published October 16, 2026

For years the polite exit from a WhatsApp call was "I don't have WhatsApp." On October 14, 2026 that excuse got weaker. WhatsApp launched guest calling: you create a call link, send it to someone, and they can join a voice or video call without installing the app or making an account.

The company posted the details on the WhatsApp Blog the same day, and the official @WhatsApp account on X confirmed the rollout with a short "Guest Calling is here" note. PCMag, The Verge, and MacRumors covered the same launch within hours.

What guest calling is

According to WhatsApp's blog post:

  • Anyone with a WhatsApp account can create a call link and invite people who do not have WhatsApp.
  • Guests join free calls that are end-to-end encrypted and have no time limit.
  • Guests can join one-to-one calls and group calls with up to 32 people.
  • Only someone with a WhatsApp account can create the link. Guests accept invites; they do not start the call.

That last point matters for mixed groups. A host who already uses WhatsApp can pull in a contractor, a relative abroad, or a classmate who never installed the app, without asking them to register first.

How to create and share a call link

WhatsApp's steps, also confirmed by PCMag's walkthrough:

  1. Open WhatsApp and go to the Calls tab.
  2. Tap the + icon.
  3. Choose New call link (or create a call link, depending on the label on your build).
  4. Copy the link and send it however you like: WhatsApp chat, email, SMS, calendar invite body, or another messenger.

PCMag checked that hosts can share the link from WhatsApp on a phone and from WhatsApp on the web. You do not need a special "guest mode" account on your side. You need a normal WhatsApp account and a current app or Web session with the Calls tab.

How a guest joins

From the WhatsApp Blog:

  1. The guest opens the link in a computer browser.
  2. They type in a name.
  3. They land in a waiting room.
  4. The host admits them before they appear on the call.

Until the host approves, the guest is not on the call. The host can also remove a guest later. That waiting room is the same idea WhatsApp already used for approved group call links earlier in 2026; guest calling extends it to people with no account at all.

If the link does nothing on a phone browser, try a laptop or desktop first. WhatsApp's own post points guests at a computer browser. PCMag's updated note adds that mobile guests who do get into a video call cannot share their own camera the way desktop guests can. Treat a computer as the reliable join path at launch.

What guests can do on the call

WhatsApp says guests get the core in-call tools:

  • Screen sharing from their computer, so they can walk through a document or slide deck.
  • Emoji reactions (WhatsApp names thumbs up and heart).
  • Raise hand, so they can signal they want to speak.

The Verge and MacRumors repeat those same guest features in their Oct 14 coverage. Guests are not second-class for the basics of a meeting-style call: they can show a screen, react, and ask to talk.

What guests cannot do

WhatsApp is explicit about the guest limits:

  • Guests cannot see other participants' phone numbers.
  • Guests cannot send messages in the call context described in the announcement.
  • Guests cannot add other people directly to the call.
  • Guests need the host's approval to enter, and the host can remove them.

Those rules keep a shared link from becoming an open party. Someone who forwards your link still hits the waiting room. Someone who joins still does not get your number from the call UI.

Limits worth knowing before you rely on it

A few practical edges from the launch coverage:

  • Host required. No WhatsApp account means you cannot mint your own link. You need a WhatsApp user to start.
  • Browser join for guests. Plan on a computer for people without the app. Do not assume a phone-only guest has a smooth path yet.
  • Rollout. Meta said guest calling began rolling out on Oct 14. If your Calls tab has no New call link option, update WhatsApp and check again over the next days.
  • Calendar plugins later. WhatsApp says Google and Outlook calendar integrations arrive later this year. You will install a WhatsApp plugin, connect your account, and drop a call link into a calendar invite so people with or without WhatsApp can join. That is not live in the Oct 14 announcement; do not wait for it if you need a call this week. Create the link from the Calls tab and paste it into the invite yourself.

When guest calling helps (and when it does not)

Good fits:

  • A work check-in where one person refuses to install another chat app.
  • Family video with someone who only uses FaceTime or Zoom.
  • A tutoring or client call where you already live in WhatsApp and they do not.

Weaker fits:

  • You need the guest to stay in a chat thread after the call. Guest calling is for the call, not for ongoing messaging without an account.
  • Everyone already has WhatsApp. A normal call or an existing call link for WhatsApp users is enough.
  • The guest only has a locked-down phone and no computer. Wait for clearer mobile guest support, or pick another tool for that person.

Quick checklist

Before you send the link:

  1. Update WhatsApp so the Calls tab shows call-link creation.
  2. Create the link from Calls → + → New call link.
  3. Tell guests to open it on a computer browser and type their name.
  4. Stay in the call long enough to admit people from the waiting room.
  5. Remind guests they will not see phone numbers and cannot add others.

Guest calling does not replace Zoom, Meet, or FaceTime for every meeting. It does remove the "install WhatsApp first" step for a lot of one-off calls. For hosts who already run their day inside WhatsApp, that is the useful change from the Oct 14 launch.
