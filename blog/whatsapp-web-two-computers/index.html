Can you use WhatsApp Web on two computers at the same time?

Published November 20, 2026 · Em português

Yes. WhatsApp Web on a computer counts as a linked device, and WhatsApp lets you link up to four devices at a time to one account (WhatsApp Help Center). The four are on top of the phone you registered with. So a work laptop and a home desktop can both have WhatsApp Web open, and new messages arrive on both.

Each linked device connects to WhatsApp on its own, with the same end-to-end encryption as your phone, and keeps working when the phone is offline (WhatsApp Help Center).

Link the second computer

  1. On the second computer, open web.whatsapp.com. A QR code appears.
  2. On an iPhone, open WhatsApp and tap Settings or your profile picture, then Linked devices > Link device. On Android, tap the three-dot menu, then Linked devices > Link a device.
  3. Unlock with Face ID, Touch ID, your fingerprint or your phone PIN if asked, and point the phone at the QR code (WhatsApp Help Center).

The first computer stays logged in. If WhatsApp doesn't recognise the new device, it asks you to confirm with Link device or Don't link. You can also link with your phone number instead of the QR code, which our post on a QR code that won't scan walks through.

Two tabs in one browser

Inside one browser, WhatsApp Web runs in one tab at a time. Open it in a second tab and that tab says WhatsApp is open in another window, with a Use here button that moves it to the tab in front of you. A different browser, or a second browser profile, keeps its own storage, so it shows a fresh QR code. Once you link it, it takes one of your four slots.

What each computer gets from WhatsApp

  • New messages and calls. They arrive on every linked device.
  • Older messages, maybe not all of them. WhatsApp's linking page lists a known issue where some linked devices don't show up to one year of chat history, and says the full history is still on your phone (WhatsApp Help Center). If a chat looks cut off on the new computer, see WhatsApp Web not showing old messages.
  • Not everything. Linked devices can't show a live location, create or view broadcast lists, or use WhatsApp Flows with businesses (WhatsApp Help Center).

Both computers depend on your phone. Linked devices log out if you haven't used WhatsApp on the phone in 14 days, and WhatsApp disconnects a linked device after 30 days without use. The computer you only open on Fridays is the one that drops first. Our post on why WhatsApp Web keeps logging out has the rest.

What stays on each computer

WhatsApp syncs your account. Some things belong to the browser on each computer and stay there. Files you download or export land in that computer's Downloads folder. Desktop notifications are a browser permission, so you allow them separately on each computer. Chrome extensions that keep their data in the browser start empty on the second computer.

That last one applies to our extension. WA Folders keeps folders, notes and its search index in Chrome on the computer where you made them, so a second computer starts with no folders. There are two ways to move them:

  • Backup and restore. In the WA Folders settings page, Download backup saves everything to a file. On the other computer, choose the file and Restore, either merging with what's there or replacing it. The search index is left out unless you tick the box to include it, because that part contains message text. The file doesn't carry your Pro license.
  • Chrome Sync (Pro). If you turn it on, it copies folder names, colours, which chats are in each folder (chat names only), quick replies and rules to your other Chrome browsers through your own Google account. Messages, notes, reminders and the search index never leave the computer.

When you stop using a computer

Log it out from your phone. Open Linked devices, tap the computer, then Log out (WhatsApp Help Center). Check that list now and then. A work PC you left behind can still show your chats until you log it out. For a computer you can't get back to, such as a hotel PC, see left WhatsApp Web logged in on someone else's computer.
